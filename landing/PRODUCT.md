# VibeMusic Landing

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective users evaluating VibeMusic as their local music player. They've heard about it through GitHub, open-source communities, or word of mouth. They want to understand what it does, whether it supports their OS, and whether it respects their privacy — before downloading.

## Product Purpose

A marketing landing page that explains, showcases, and drives downloads of the VibeMusic desktop app. It exists to convert visitors into users by demonstrating feature depth, design quality, and open-source integrity in one scrolling session.

## Brand Personality

Precise, confident, intentional — same as the app. The landing page carries the same design DNA so the download feels like a continuation, not a transition.

Three words: **Precise · Confident · Welcoming**

## Anti-references

- **Not AI-template** — no gradient text (except the deliberate hero gradient), no glass cards, no numbered section markers (01 / 02 / 03), no tiny uppercase eyebrows above every section ("FEATURES", "DOWNLOAD"), no stock photography.
- **Not a feature list** — not a wall of text with icons. Each feature is a distinct layout block, not a card grid.
- **Not a SaaS landing** — no pricing tiers, no "start free trial", no cookie-cutter hero-metric layout.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** Screenshots and interactive previews do more work than copy. Each feature section has a visual anchor (screenshot, mockup, or animation).
2. **Same design DNA as the app.** Visitors who download should feel like they've already arrived — same color language, same typography, same attention to detail.
3. **One job: download.** The hero and CTA lead here. Secondary actions (GitHub, features, About) support but never distract.
4. **Platform pride.** Cross-platform support (Windows, macOS, Linux) isn't a footnote — it's a brand signal. The landing should wear the platform badges visibly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.2 AA**. Landing page contrast requirements match the app: body ≥4.5:1 against all surfaces. Hero copy on any gradient or image must pass contrast independently. Reduced-motion fallbacks for entrance animations (crossfade vs. slide-up stagger). Clear focus indicators on all interactive elements.
